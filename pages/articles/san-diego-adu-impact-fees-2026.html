San Diego ADU Impact Fees: What You'll Actually Pay in 2026

Last updated: May 2026

Many San Diego homeowners assume an ADU permit is just a few thousand dollars. In reality, utility charges, school fees, sewer costs, and site-specific requirements can dramatically change the final budget.

Direct Answer

In 2026, many San Diego detached ADUs still qualify for partial fee reductions or exemptions, especially under California's 750 square foot rule.

However, permit costs can still become significant due to sewer upgrades, utility connections, grading, coastal review, canyon lot engineering, fire access, or bonus ADU requirements.

Some homeowners spend under $10,000 on permitting and fees. Others encounter total pre-construction soft costs exceeding $40,000 before construction even starts.

Typical San Diego ADU Fees in 2026

Fee Type Typical Range
Building permit fees $3,000 - $8,000+
Sewer connection / capacity fees $0 - $15,000+

Actual costs depend heavily on lot conditions, utility setup, overlays, and project scope.

What Are ADU Impact Fees?

"Impact fees" are charges cities or agencies may collect to offset the effect of new housing on public infrastructure and services.

Depending on the project, San Diego ADU-related costs may involve:

  • School district fees
  • Traffic or transportation impact fees
  • Sewer capacity charges
  • Water infrastructure upgrades
  • Stormwater review
  • Utility connection work

Some fees are charged directly by the city. Others may come from utility providers or separate public agencies.

The California 750 Sq Ft Rule

California state law limits certain impact fees for ADUs under 750 square feet.

This is one reason many San Diego homeowners intentionally target ADU designs around 500–749 square feet.

Important: "Impact fee exemption" does not always mean "zero fees." Utility upgrades, sewer work, plan check costs, and site engineering can still apply.

In practice, the 750 sqft threshold can significantly improve project feasibility for detached backyard ADUs.

Which Fees Often Still Apply?

Even when certain development impact fees are reduced, homeowners may still face major infrastructure-related expenses.

  • Electrical panel upgrades
  • Separate utility trenching
  • Water pressure upgrades
  • Sewer lateral replacement
  • Stormwater compliance
  • Fire sprinkler requirements

Older San Diego properties sometimes trigger expensive utility corrections once permitting begins.

Related research: fire sprinkler requirements , parking near transit , and coastal permits .

Coastal and Canyon Lots Can Cost More

Two San Diego properties with identical ADU floor plans may end up with very different permitting costs.

Coastal overlays, environmentally sensitive land, steep slopes, retaining walls, geotechnical engineering, and wildfire access rules can all increase expenses.

Canyon lots especially may require:

  • Additional grading review
  • Drainage analysis
  • Geotechnical reports
  • Fire access clearance
  • Biological review
This is one reason online "average ADU cost" estimates are often misleading. Site conditions matter more than many homeowners expect.

How to Estimate Fees Before Designing

Many homeowners spend thousands on plans before discovering major zoning or infrastructure problems.

Before committing to full architectural drawings, try to confirm:

  • Zoning and overlay conditions
  • Coastal permit requirements
  • Transit Priority Area status
  • Slope or canyon constraints
  • Utility access conditions
  • Potential sewer upgrade requirements

Early feasibility research can prevent expensive redesigns later.
