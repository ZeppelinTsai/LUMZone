Can I Build an ADU in San Diego Without an Architect? | Permit & Design Guide

Last updated: May 2026

You can start ADU research without hiring an architect. But once the project moves from idea to permit, San Diego will need a real building permit package with plans, code information, and sometimes engineering.

Best use of LUMZone: screen the address, zoning, ADU type, size, parking, setbacks, height, and obvious red flags before spending thousands on design.

Direct Answer

No, you do not need an architect just to ask whether an ADU might work on your San Diego property. Early feasibility can be done with zoning records, parcel information, city bulletins, and a clear description of the project.

But yes, you should expect professional help before permit approval if the ADU involves new construction, structural changes, complex hillside conditions, fire access issues, utility changes, or custom drawings.

What You Can Do Yourself

  • Confirm the property is in the City of San Diego or another jurisdiction.
  • Check the zone, overlays, coastal status, and lot constraints.
  • Estimate whether the ADU is a conversion, attached unit, detached unit, or JADU.
  • Research likely size, setback, parking, and height issues.
  • Compare a custom design with a preapproved plan option.

Where Architects or Engineers Usually Matter

San Diego ADU submittals typically need site plans, floor plans, roof plans, elevations, sections, structural details, structural calculations when applicable, and Title 24 energy documentation. That is where a licensed architect, engineer, designer, or experienced permit professional often becomes valuable.

For garage conversions, a simpler design team may be enough. For a new two-story detached ADU, hillside site, coastal project, or large utility upgrade, the professional scope usually grows.

Preapproved Plans

San Diego allows applicants to use preapproved ADU plans under California's preapproved plan framework. This can shorten review, but it does not erase site-specific review. The plan still has to fit the property, setbacks, foundation conditions, utilities, drainage, access, and overlays.

Smart First Step

Before hiring a design team, collect the address, APN if available, existing building records, garage dimensions, lot size, approximate slope, and whether the property is near the coast or a canyon. Then run a feasibility screen.
