San Diego ADU Setback Rules Explained

A plain-English overview of how setback rules may affect detached ADUs in San Diego. This article is for early zoning research only and should be verified with official city sources or qualified professionals.

Direct Answer

In San Diego, detached ADUs are generally allowed, but setback rules depend on the ADU’s height, location, zoning context, and whether the property is in a fire hazard or overlay area.

For many ADUs, the most important questions are whether the unit is 16 feet or less in height, whether it is located near residential property lines, and whether the property falls inside a High or Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone.

Key Setback Concepts

1. Front Yard and Street Side Yard

ADUs may need to follow the front yard or street side yard setback rules of the base zone. In some cases, smaller ADUs may be allowed to encroach into these areas, but the details depend on the applicable local rules.

2. Interior Side Yard and Rear Yard

For ADUs that are 16 feet or less in height, San Diego rules may allow reduced or no minimum interior side and rear yard setbacks, subject to building and fire safety requirements.

For ADUs that are over 16 feet tall or multi-story, additional setback requirements may apply, commonly including a minimum setback when adjacent to residentially zoned property or residential uses.

3. Fire Hazard Areas

If the property is located in a High or Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone, additional defensible space or setback requirements may apply. This is especially important for detached ADUs near native or naturalized vegetation.

Practical Summary

  • A detached ADU may be possible on a San Diego single-family lot.
  • ADUs 16 feet or less may have more flexible side and rear setback treatment.
  • Taller or two-story ADUs may trigger stricter setback requirements.
  • Fire hazard zones and overlay districts can change the analysis.
  • Building code and fire safety rules may require more clearance than zoning rules alone suggest.
Even if zoning setbacks appear flexible, fire separation, opening protection, utility access, drainage, and site-specific conditions may still affect whether the ADU can be approved.

Questions to Ask Before Designing

  • Is the property in San Diego city jurisdiction?
  • Is the ADU detached, attached, or a conversion?
  • Will the ADU be 16 feet or less, or taller?
  • Is the site in a Coastal Overlay Zone?
  • Is the site in a High or Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone?
  • Does the design include exterior stairs, decks, or balconies?
